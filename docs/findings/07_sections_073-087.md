## 75. A frequency-table piece -- design registered before implementing

Section 74 left count_unique at about 25/30 with the generic nested
piece, and showed that *replacing* draws can cost other problems. This
section *adds* one stateful piece. It is the classic "count per element"
table, not a count_unique shape.

**`--combinator-frequency`, opt-in, byte-identical when off.** It adds a
fifth stateful piece, `FrequencyTable`, drawn uniformly with the other
four when three loop slots are free. It runs on the whole-array or
length-prefixed layout:
1. **Table:** `aux[i]` = the number of j with cmp(elem(j), elem(i)),
   with cmp one of `==`, `<`, `>`. That gives the frequency table, or
   the rank of each element counted from below or from above.
2. **Pass over the table:** one existing single-pass relation over
   `aux[i]`. The relation is one of four:
   - the largest aux;
   - the smallest aux;
   - count-if `aux[i] cmp2 thr`;
   - exists `aux[i] cmp2 thr`.

   cmp2 is any of the six comparators, and thr is one of 0, 1, 2 or n/2.

It removes nothing, so every draw that used to solve has_majority is
still there.

**Protocol (as in section 71, with section 72's rule).**
1. Implement and freeze.
2. Write post-freeze problems and register predictions.
3. A/B over every registered array/scalar problem: base (current
   default) vs + `--combinator-frequency`, 10 matched seeds,
   `--step-limit 20000`, per-seed `--benchmark`.

Phase-1 sampling is reported as a diagnostic only. Time is not a gate.

## 77. A pointer-following piece -- design registered before implementing

cycle is the last of section 70's five. It needs a loop whose next index
comes from the data. Per the standing rule (generic pieces before
skeletons), this is a new combinator family, not a cycle skeleton. This
also retires cycle as the held-out "ruler" it has been since sections 25
and 38. The boundaries below take over that role.

**`--combinator-follow`, opt-in, byte-identical when off.** With the flag
on, one combinator block in eight is a follow block.

- **Layout.** Node i's pointer is `arr[base + i]`.
  - `base = 1` with `start = arr[0]`: head-prefixed, cycle's encoding.
  - `base = 0` with `start = 0`.
  - `nodes = v0 - base`. A pointer outside `[0, nodes)` ends the walk,
    which acts as the null.
- **Loop.** `p = start; steps = 0; while (steps < v0 && 0 <= p < nodes)
  { relation; p = arr[base + p]; steps++ }`. The cap of v0 steps is the
  pigeonhole bound.
- **Relation**, one of:
  - `CountSteps`: acc = steps;
  - `StillInRange`: acc = 1 if p is still in range after the loop, i.e.
    the walk never ended;
  - `VisitsKey`: acc = 1 if p == k at some step, k one of 0..2;
  - `TwoSpeed`: a second pointer q advances two steps per iteration,
    each step range-checked; acc = 1 if p == q after a move (Floyd).

**Protocol** (sections 71-76, with section 72's rule):
1. Implement and freeze.
2. Write post-freeze problems on cycle's encoding and register
   predictions.
3. A/B: cycle and the post-freeze problems at 30 seeds, plus all
   registered array/scalar problems at 10 seeds for no-regression.

Time is not a gate.

## 80. Orthogonal modifiers: difference direction and absolute value as axes -- design registered before implementing

The first of section 79's four gaps. Two modifiers exist today only as
special cases of single relations:
- **direction**: the nested `BestDiff` uses `elem(j) - elem(i)` only;
- **absolute value**: `|step|` exists only inside `BestStep`.

That asymmetry is what keeps max_drawdown and total_variation out of
reach (sections 71 and 79). `--combinator-orthogonal` (opt-in,
byte-identical when off) turns both into axes:
- **Direction** applies to every difference-based relation: nested
  `BestDiff`, and the adjacent step relations `BestStep` and `SumStep`.
  Each draws `b - a` or `a - b`, half and half.
- **`SumStep`** is a new adjacent relation: acc += step. It is the step
  counterpart of `Sum`, drawn as a step relation alongside `BestStep`
  and `CountSegments`.
- **Absolute value** applies one time in three to both step relations
  (already true for `BestStep` since section 71).

No new loop or layout: the change only multiplies options inside
existing relations.

**Protocol** (as in sections 71-79):
1. Freeze.
2. Write post-freeze problems with registered predictions, including at
   least one designed boundary.
3. A/B: base vs + `--combinator-orthogonal` on every registered
   array/scalar problem, 10 matched seeds; drops of 2 on
   second_smallest or max_gap_sorted are retested at 30 seeds (section
   76) before counting.

Time is not a gate.

## 82. Arg-best: returning *where* the best is, not its value -- design registered before implementing

The second of section 79's gaps. Every best-type aggregate returns the
best *value*:
- the plain single-loop `Best`, which yields the element itself;
- the nested outer `Best`, which yields the best t_i;
- `FrequencyTable`'s max/min pass, which yields the best aux.

None returns the element (or index) at which the best occurs, so `mode`
is out of reach. `--combinator-argbest` (opt-in, byte-identical when off)
adds an arg variant to those three aggregates, drawn half the time when
the flag is on:
- **Returns** `elem(i)` or `i` (the plain Best returns `i` only, since
  its value is already the element).
- **Ties** follow one of three rules:
  - first wins (strict `>` / `<`);
  - last wins (non-strict);
  - the smaller element wins (`better || (equal && elem(i) < arg)`).

This is the classic argmax. It is one modifier on existing aggregates,
not a new family.

**Protocol** (sections 71-81):
1. Freeze.
2. Write post-freeze problems. *Before registering gain predictions,
   run the current default on each one (10 seeds) and report what it
   already solves (section 81's rule).*
3. A/B on every registered array/scalar problem, 10 matched seeds.
   Drops of 2 on second_smallest or max_gap_sorted are retested at 30
   seeds.

## 86. First-occurrence filter: counting values, not elements -- design registered before implementing

The third of section 79's gaps. The frequency table (section 75) counts
*elements*: a count-if over `aux[i] == 2` counts both copies of a value
that occurs twice. values_twice needs each *value* once.

**`--combinator-first-occurrence`** (opt-in, byte-identical when off).
On the frequency table's count-if and exists passes, half the time with
the flag on, the table keeps `aux[i]` only at a value's first
occurrence. The inner loop also notes whether an equal element appeared
at some j < i; if so, `aux[i] = 0`. Every existing threshold test then
counts distinct values instead of elements: `aux == 2`, `aux >= 2`,
`aux > 0`, ... The max/min passes are left alone, since zeros would
break a minimum.

This is one modifier on one existing piece. "Deduplicate by first
occurrence" is the classic way to go from elements to values.

**Protocol** (sections 71-85):
1. Freeze.
2. Write post-freeze problems.
3. Run a base check (section 81's rule) before registering predictions.
4. A/B on every registered array/scalar problem, 10 matched seeds.
   Drops of 2 on second_smallest, max_gap_sorted or min_gap (section
   85's watch item) are retested at 30 seeds.

## 87. Distinct counting, v2 -- design registered before implementing

Section 86's first-occurrence filter had two defects: a 0-sentinel that
collides with `<=` tests, and a share taken from the unfiltered count-if
pass. v2 replaces it. The v1 code and flag are removed; section 86's
batteries need commit 1861fae to rerun.

**`--combinator-distinct`** (opt-in, byte-identical when off) adds a sixth
stateful piece, `DistinctCount`, drawn as one more equal option among the
stateful pieces when two loops are free. The other pieces keep their own
slots, so no single pass is halved. In a single nested pass:

```
for each i:
    t = #{ j : elem(j) == elem(i) }
    seen = exists j < i with elem(j) == elem(i)
    if (seen == 0) { if (t cmp2 thr) acc += 1 (count) | acc = 1 (exists) }
```

- No sentinel and no aux: repeats are *skipped*, so every threshold
  test means what it says.
- cmp2 is one of the six comparators; thr is one of 0, 1, 2, n/2; the
  aggregate is count or exists.

**Targets:**
- values_twice;
- repeated_values;
- rare_values (all three written before this freeze).

**Protocol:**
1. Freeze.
2. Write new post-freeze problems.
3. Base check (section 81).
4. A/B on every registered array/scalar problem, 10 matched seeds,
   with 30-seed retests of drops of 2 on second_smallest,
   max_gap_sorted, min_gap and elements_twice.

### 87, investigation. min_gap's losses are wall timeouts plus a standing n = 1 gap, not repeat-detectors

The hypothesis from the retest (repeat-detecting blocks as attractors) is
**refuted**. Not one champion of a failing min_gap run in either arm
contains an element `==` test or an `aux` table. The failures break down
as follows (30-seed retest, `data/distinct_retest_20261009/`):

| failure cause | base | DC |
|---|---|---|
| rejected: "failed exhaustively at n=1" | 8 | 9 |
| wall timeout (180 s) | 2 | 6 |
| other | 0 | 0 |
| **total failures** | **10** | **15** |

1. **The extra losses under DC are wall-clock timeouts.** DC's finished
   min_gap runs average 27.5 s against base's 18.8 s, so more of them
   cross the 180 s wall. That is a time cost. By section 62's principle
   (time is not a gate), it is not a reachability loss.
2. **About a third of min_gap runs fail in both arms on an n = 1 gap.**
   - The rejected champions are the correct sort-then-min-step program,
     53/53 at N = 8, but mutation dropped the accumulator's `= 0`
     initialization.
   - Training starts at n = 2, so nothing ever tests the single-element
     case.
   - With n = 1, the program returns whatever the sort left in that
     variable, and the verifier's exhaustive n = 1 check rejects it.
   - max_gap_sorted shows the same failure (4 and 3 seeds), which is why
     both siblings swing so much at 10 seeds.

   This is a standing problem-generator gap, independent of any piece.

**Consequences.**
- Sections 83 and 87's min_gap "losses" were measured through these two
  artifacts: a wall budget and an untrained edge case. Neither is a
  reachability cost of the pieces.
- Follow-up, not done: training levels for array/scalar problems should
  include n = 1 (or verification and training should agree on the
  smallest n). That should remove most of min_gap's and
  max_gap_sorted's variance.
