## 126. PSB2, the comparable baseline -- design and predictions registered before running

Why: section 16 compared us to PSB2 only by order of magnitude (our evaluation counts against its 60M cap), on
different problems. `docs/literature.md` names PSB2 (Helmuth and Kelly, GECCO 2021) as the nearest published
non-neural benchmark: PushGP with lexicase selection, 100 runs per problem, 200 training cases (every hand-chosen
edge case, then random ones), 2,000 unseen test cases, at most 60,000,000 program executions, success = every
training and test case passes. Owner's rule: distance from LLM-guided systems is intended; closeness to comparable
non-neural work is what must be measured.

Adapter (`Psb2Problem`, `scripts/psb2_prepare.sh`, `scripts/run_psb2.sh`, `--max-executions N`, `make test`
238/238 plus three PSB2 tests): the official datasets are downloaded (edge files whole, the first ~6 MB of each
random file) and sampled by the run's seed, as PSB2 does. The training set is returned for every level, so
`--max-n 1` is one PSB2 run; the 2,000 test cases are the adversarial set, so final_status SUCCESS with all
adversarial cases passed is PSB2's definition. The reference function of each problem was checked against 70,000 to
160,000 official cases per problem, 0 mismatches. Seven of the 25 problems fit the integer-array interface without
changing their meaning (Fuel Cost, Basement, GCD, Find Pair, Coin Sums, Luhn, Paired Digits); the other 18 need
strings, floats or vector outputs and are not run. Encodings: see `Psb2Problem.hpp`.

Differences from PSB2, stated so nobody over-reads the comparison: the instruction set is Mini-C, not Push (so
no per-type stacks, but also no lists or strings); the budget is counted as candidates scored x training cases, an
upper bound on executions (failing candidates stop early); our code was designed over sections 1-125 and GCD is one
of the problems it was tuned on, so GCD is contaminated and the other six are the held-out comparison; 30 runs per
problem here, not 100 (a first pass; the confidence interval is wide, about +-18 points at 50%).

Protocol: seeds 70000-70029, current default configuration, `--max-n 1 --num-vars 7 --threads 1 --beam-width 500
--candidates 1500 --max-program-size 150 --step-limit 20000 --max-executions 60000000`, wall cap 900 s as a safety.

PushGP + lexicase success out of 100 (PSB2 table): Fuel Cost 50, Find Pair 4, GCD 8, Basement 1, Coin Sums 2, Paired
Digits 8, Luhn 0.

bloated code (loops over unrelated variables that happen not to disturb the answer, and for even lengths the second cell passes
by the leftover `swap`/`arr[1]` writes); none uses a term-expr, prefix-cross, pair-find, parity-term, sum-if or radix block. A 2/20
vs 0/20 difference has Fisher p about 0.24, so it is noise at this size.

Net: the second round also gives **no evidence that the pieces generalize**: three of four problems are out of reach for both arms
and the fourth is at a 0-10% rate. The reachable unseen-problem pool in PSB2 is exhausted for this encoding style (floats and
free strings are excluded). Any real test needs new unseen problems that are solvable, e.g. PSB1-style problems with
self-written references.

## 136. Why count_odds and sum_of_squares stay at 0/20 in the medium PSB1-style test (diagnosis; no code change)

Question from section 135: the two simplest unsolved problems. Findings, from the logs of seed 74000 (60 s with `ALGOSEARCH_DEBUG_BEST=1`) and from the generator source:

- **Both are expressible** (new test `psb1_medium_count_odds_and_sum_of_squares_are_expressible_in_the_grammar`: hand-written programs of
  about 15 statements pass all 2,000 unseen cases). So the failure is search reach, not the language or the encoding.
- **count_odds** (best 87/200, size 85): the beam sits on a plateau of nonsense (count of elements below the median through a double
  loop). The needed core is `if (arr[i] % 2 != 0) acc = acc + 1;`. In the combinator, `CountIf` builds the test as
  `cmp(elem, rhs)` with `rhs` the key or another element (`ProgramGenerator.cpp`, `case Relation::CountIf`): the element is
  compared raw, never under `% c`. A `% 2` test appears only inside the distinct-aggregate variant (`t % 2 == p` on a
  frequency count) and in parity-term (an `i % 2` position test). So "count the items satisfying a predicate of the item" is
  outside the combinator's CountIf family; it can only come from free mutation.
- **sum_of_squares** (best 47/200, size 37): the best program is a divisor-sum (`while (v4 <= arr[0]) if (arr[0] % v4 == 0)`), i.e. the
  scalar-loop `Test` body. The scalar-loop `Fold` body is `a = a {+, -, *} {i, X, Y, arr[i]}`: it has the loop bound `i * i <= B` but no
  `i * i` **term** in the body, so `a = a + i * i` is not in the piece. (The input is a single cell, so the combinator's v0-bounded
  loops do not apply either; the scalar loop is the only structured route and it lacks the term.)
- Both gaps are structural and generic ("a predicate or a product of the loop variable inside an existing accumulator"), not
  specific to these two names.

What is not claimed: that adding the missing forms would solve them (not run), or that it would help anywhere else. Any such change
would be designed after seeing these two problems, so it needs the same unseen-problem protocol as sections 132-135 (and those
showed that the earlier pieces did not transfer); registered as an option, not a result.

## 137. Closing the two gaps of section 136: `--count-term` and `--square-term` (opt-in), reach result, and the registered tests

Two options, both 0 by default (no RNG draw, byte-identical): `--count-term P` makes a single-loop combinator `CountIf` test a term
of the element against the key (`drawTermExpr`: x % c, x / c, x * c, x +- c, ...) instead of the raw element; `--square-term P`
lets the scalar-loop `Fold` accumulate `i * i` as its term. They were designed after seeing count_odds and sum_of_squares, so the
reach result below proves only that the gap is closed, not that the options help in general.

Reach (not a test of generality): matched seeds 74000-74009 of section 135, 300 s, defaults plus `--count-term 0.3 --square-term
0.3` (`data/psb1m_reach_20261022`), against 0/20 in section 135: sum_of_squares **10/10**, count_odds **1/10**. The solved programs
are the clean ones (`while (v1 <= arr[0]) { v2 = v2 + v1 * v1; ... }`; `if (arr[v4] % 2 != 0) v5 = v5 + 1`). sum_of_squares is
fixed; count_odds is reachable but rare (the draw needs layout Whole, a single loop, CountIf, the option, the form x % 2, the
comparator != and the key 0, a product of small probabilities).

The probability used from here on is **0.1** for both, not the 0.3 of the reach run (lesson of section 131: high P cost
elements_twice); reach at 0.1 has not been measured.

New problems, written and fixed before running anything with the options (`psb1n_<name>`, medium style): count_multiples_of_three,
even_squares, sum_of_cubes, for_loop_index, smallest4 (definitions in `Psb1Problem.hpp`; unit-tested). They are 5 problems the
options were not designed for; count_multiples_of_three shares count_odds's mechanism on purpose, so it is the one place where a
gain is expected.

Two tests, run one after the other (`scripts/run_count_square_joint_battery.sh`, then the chain below):
1. **Regression battery**, the 77 suite problems, 10 matched seeds 60100-60109, base (current defaults) vs `--count-term 0.1
   --square-term 0.1`. Rule (sections 99/131): no per-problem drop of 2 or more, aggregate not worse.
2. **Unseen set**: the 5 new problems, base vs the two options at 0.1, matched seeds 75000-75019, 300 s, 60M executions, 200
   training and 2,000 unseen cases.

Predictions (written before the runs):
- Battery: passes the rule (aggregate within +-3 of base, no drop of 2). If a drop of 2 or more appears it is reported and the
  options stay opt-in.
- Unseen: count_multiples_of_three NEW >= BASE + 2 (but at most 8/20: count_odds was only 1/10 even at P = 0.3); sum_of_cubes,
  even_squares, for_loop_index and smallest4: |NEW - BASE| <= 2 (the options do not touch them); no harm anywhere, NEW >= BASE - 2.
- If the only difference is on count_multiples_of_three, the claim is limited to "one extra predicate form closes one gap", not
  generality.

### 137 (results). Battery: 760 vs 758 with one drop of 2; unseen set: no gain on count_multiples_of_three, nothing else moves

**Battery** (`data/count_square_joint_battery_20261022`, 77 problems, seeds 60100-60109; "solved" = at least one genuinely
generalizing solution kept): base 760, `--count-term 0.1 --square-term 0.1` 758. The only problem that changed is majority_value, 5 -> 3
(a drop of 2). The prediction "aggregate within +-3" holds; "no drop of 2" **fails**, so the options stay opt-in under the rule of
sections 99/131. majority_value is noisy on this suite (section 131 had it 2 -> 5 with no explanation); a 30-seed repeat to separate
noise from cost was not run.

**Unseen set** (`data/psb1n_{base,new}_20261022`, seeds 75000-75019, 300 s; solved = 2,000/2,000):

| problem | BASE | NEW (0.1 / 0.1) |
|---|---|---|
| smallest4 | 20/20 | 20/20 |
| even_squares | 1/20 | 2/20 |
| sum_of_cubes | 1/20 | 2/20 |
| count_multiples_of_three | 0/20 | 0/20 |
| for_loop_index | 0/20 | 0/20 |

Against the predictions: the main one **failed**: count_multiples_of_three is 0/20 in both arms (predicted NEW >= BASE + 2); its best
partial scores in the NEW arm are 104-105/200, nowhere near solved. The other four satisfy |NEW - BASE| <= 2, and there is no harm
(NEW >= BASE - 2 everywhere). The few solved runs of even_squares and sum_of_cubes (1-2 of 20, in both arms) are bloated programs
(many dead loops) that pass because their training and test domains are tiny (n in 1..8 gives 8 distinct inputs for sum_of_cubes);
they are chance solutions, not evidence for or against the options.

Reading: the options close the two gaps they were built for on the two problems that motivated them (reach run: sum_of_squares 10/10 at
P = 0.3, count_odds 1/10), but at the registered P = 0.1 the same mechanism does **not** carry over to count_multiples_of_three, the
closest sibling of count_odds. The reach on count_odds was already low (1/10 at 0.3); the form needed there is one among several
dozen term forms times the comparator and the key, so at 0.1 it is rarer still. This is a fourth negative on transfer (sections
132-135 were three), and a more specific one: a piece tuned to one problem's exact form does not reach a sibling that needs a
different constant. A fix would be a narrower, higher-yield form (predicate `x % c == 0` or `!= 0` with c drawn directly, instead of the
whole term set), which is again a design made after seeing results.

Decisions: both options stay opt-in; no promotion. Paper-worthiness: unchanged.
